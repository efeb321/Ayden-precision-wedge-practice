GCQuad numbers guide

These are general elite-wedge reference ranges, not hard rules. Ayden's own repeated numbers over months of data matter more than any single target here — this is just so he knows what each number is and roughly where it should live.

Face to Target · controls start line

Where the clubface points at impact relative to the target line. This is the single biggest factor in which direction the ball starts — more important than path. Target: within 1–2° of 0.

Launch Direction · confirms start line

The actual direction the ball left the face. This is the real result — face angle is the cause, side angle is the effect. Target: as close to 0° as possible.

Club path · shot shape

The direction the clubhead is moving through impact. Path relative to face angle is what curves the ball left or right after it starts. Near 0° for a stock, straight shot.

Ball speed · drives distance

How fast the ball leaves the face. There's no universal target number — the goal is that Ayden's ball speed repeats tightly every time he hits the same planned distance. Big swings in ball speed for the same shot usually mean inconsistent strike.

Attack angle · strike quality

Whether the club is moving down or up into the ball. A slightly descending strike compresses the ball and produces clean, spinny contact. Typical wedge range: −4° to −8°.

Dynamic loft · trajectory and spin source

The actual loft delivered at impact, which can differ from the club's stamped loft depending on hands and shaft lean. Should track close to the club's listed loft — a big gap usually means strike or setup is off.

Launch angle · trajectory

The vertical angle the ball leaves the face. Combined with spin, this sets how the ball flies and how it lands. Typical full wedge range: 24–34° — lower for a knockdown, higher for a high, soft shot.

Spin Rate · stopping power

Backspin rate. Higher spin means a steeper landing angle and more stopping power on the green, but too much can rob distance. Typical full wedge range: 7,000–10,000+ rpm.

Descent Angle · will it stop

How steeply the ball is falling when it lands — the direct answer to whether a shot will stop or release. Shaped by launch angle, spin, and dynamic loft together. Steeper (higher number) means more stopping power. Only tracked in the Flight-Window drill, where trajectory shape is the point.

Which numbers matter for which skill

Start line: Face to Target first, Launch Direction confirms it, path shapes the curve.

Distance control: ball speed, attack angle, dynamic loft.

Trajectory: launch angle, dynamic loft, spin.

Spin / stopping power: Spin Rate, attack angle, dynamic loft, descent angle.
